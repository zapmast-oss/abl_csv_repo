ABL Review Franchise History Pittsburgh Express

The Express That Came
One Win Short

Twice Pittsburgh reached the Grand Series. Twice it carried the championship to Game 7. The Express built nearly everything required for a dynasty—except the crown.

By ABL Historical Desk

The Pittsburgh Express became an October fixture at their peak, compiling a 25–20 postseason record through 1980. Editorial illustration.

There is a particular kind of heartbreak reserved for teams that never get close. Pittsburgh’s was different. The Express got close enough to see the trophy, close enough to force a deciding game with everything at stake—and close enough to make the losses last forever.

Across nine completed seasons from 1972 through 1980, Pittsburgh went 747–711, won four division titles and reached the playoffs four times. The franchise’s defining run came under manager Alex Flores in 1974 and ’75, when a former 68-win club transformed into a championship threat.

The First Heartbreak

The 1974 Express won 91 games, then dispatched St. Louis and Denver in five games apiece. Pittsburgh had won 11 of its first 17 postseason games when it reached the Grand Series against Miami.

After losing the first two, the Express swept three games at home and moved within one victory of the title. Miami answered twice. Game 7 ended Hurricanes 7, Express 2.

The Greatest Express

Pittsburgh returned stronger in 1975. The Express finished 103–59 with a +163 run differential—the finest season in franchise history through 1980. Adan Reid Jr. led the offense, Daniel Mejia fronted the staff and Pittsburgh entered October carrying both confidence and unfinished business.

The Express erased a two-game deficit against New York, then swept Houston to reach a second consecutive Grand Series. Against Phoenix, they again forced a seventh game. Again, the ending belonged to someone else. Phoenix won 3–1 in Pittsburgh.

Those two clubs won 194 regular-season games, captured four playoff series and came within one win of a championship twice. Antonio Serrano remained the franchise’s enduring star, later helping Pittsburgh return to October under manager Mark Averill.

Game 7, Again

The Express won the East in 1979 but lost a third winner-take-all game, 4–3 at Seattle. A 90-win club returned in 1980, only to fall to Las Vegas in five.

Pittsburgh was not a failure. Failure does not reach consecutive Grand Series or produce a winning playoff record. The Express were excellent—and that is precisely why the empty space in the trophy case still feels so large.
