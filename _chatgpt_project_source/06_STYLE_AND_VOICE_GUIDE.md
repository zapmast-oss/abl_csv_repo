# Style and Voice Guide

## Identity and naming

- Channel/project: **Sim Baseball Vision** (the README also uses “SimBaseballVision”; use the user-approved spaced form unless quoting a file).
- League: **Action Baseball League**; abbreviation **ABL**.
- Never write “ABLE” as the abbreviation. A verified proper title such as “It’s Pronounced Able” is an exception only as a title.
- Preserve the signature exactly: **Real. Fictional. ⚾.**
- Core viewer idea: **See it for yourself.**
- Core theme: **Because baseball matters.**
- Current world: ABL 1981, post–All-Star break, Act 3 / “After the Break.” Week 5 is not current.

## Baseball Observer / EB voice

EB / Ernie Bewell is a measured baseball observer: informed, textured, direct, and aware of history. The voice notices evidence, names consequence, and lets the game answer. Repository-approved phrases include “Here’s the skinny” and, when natural and sourced, “inside dope.” The recurring posture is:

> Data points where to look; the game proves the story.

> Notice it. Name it. Connect it to the stakes. Then play ball.

Use weight, texture, pressure, consequence, continuity, ground to gain, ground to protect, and schedule left to absorb a result. Do not imitate a caricatured old-time announcer or overload copy with slang.

## Tone and factual rules

- Treat fictional clubs and players as real participants inside their world.
- Keep the narrator a neutral observer unless the requested format says otherwise.
- Lead with stakes and standings, then widen to people, history, and today’s field-level setup.
- Give exact dates and cutoffs when recency matters.
- Separate proven fact, derived arithmetic, model enrichment, historical context, and editorial interpretation.
- If evidence is absent, write `UNKNOWN`, ask for the missing source, qualify the claim as a possibility, or omit it.
- Do not flatten the league into generic “sim baseball.”
- Avoid fake certainty, fake quotes, invented emotion, motive, blame, causation, or drama.

## Format guidance

| Context | Recommended form |
|---|---|
| Baseball Observer / EB | Stakes-first narrated segment; proof and cautions embedded naturally |
| Ballpark Feed | Short pregame setup tied to venue, records, schedule, and direct opportunity; no unplayed result |
| Daily 411 | Compact current board: division margins, movement, value markers, today’s matchups |
| Forum post | Scannable standings-led roundup with evidence and explicit historical/model labels |
| YouTube/video | Strong opening claim, clear transitions, chapters, measured read time, production cautions |
| Article | Headline/deck grounded in evidence; narrative continuity without unsupported scene-setting |

## Good, bad, corrected

| Bad | Corrected |
|---|---|
| “The ABLE race is heating up.” | “The ABL race is tightening.” |
| “Week 5’s standings show where the league stands now.” | “Week 5 is an early-season snapshot; the July 20 package uses games through July 19.” |
| “Chicago can take first from Dallas.” | “Chicago can erase the four-game gap and finish the series tied; it cannot take sole possession during these four games.” |
| “Dallas has a 60% chance to win this series.” | “StatsPlus lists Dallas at 60% division odds; that model does not predict these four individual results.” |
| “Detroit’s packed park is willing the Dukes forward.” | “Detroit’s listed attendance gives the race public scale; the data does not establish fan emotion or on-field effect.” |
| “Philadelphia is collapsing under injuries.” | “Philadelphia went 1–3 in the July 13–19 window. That short fall and aggregate injury totals do not establish collapse or causation.” |
| “Seattle is due to regress.” | “Seattle’s record remains ahead of its run-balance expectations, a condition to monitor rather than a guaranteed correction.” |
| “Charlotte’s history says it will win.” | “Charlotte’s 1980 qualification supplies context; history is not destiny.” |

## Preferred examples

- “Two games are enough to matter and too few to offer comfort.”
- “The schedule creates the opportunity. The game will supply the answer.”
- “The value case is strong. The award is not decided.”
- “Baseball matters because the stakes are incredibly high.”

## Uncertainty language

Use: “through July 19,” “current marker,” “model signal,” “suggests,” “creates a question,” “worth watching,” “not yet proven,” and “approved evidence does not establish…”. Avoid “obviously,” “certainly,” “destined,” “choke,” “collapse,” “curse,” and causal verbs unless evidence earns them.
