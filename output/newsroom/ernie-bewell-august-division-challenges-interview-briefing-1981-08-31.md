# Ernie Bewell’s interview briefing for Big Earl

**The Baseball Observer · Action Baseball League · August 31, 1981**  
**Conversation:** After the August Division Challenges, August 20–30  
**Verified results:** Through August 30, 1981. August 31 and later games are scheduled as of this briefing.

## The conversation in one minute

Four division leaders have a game and a half or less to spare. Charlotte is still first after Miami swept it. Dallas swept the defending champions and still has Chicago one back. Phoenix took the first series from San Francisco and leads by one. Las Vegas also leads by one, with Denver and Seattle tied behind it. The wild cards moved as much as the division races. Miami passed Detroit and leads Chicago by half a game, while Seattle joined Denver on the ABC line.

Start with Pat Watters’s two-out single in Miami on August 30. It ended a sweep and gives Earl a concrete question about the difference between keeping first place and controlling a race. Let his answer set the next subject. If he talks about head-to-head play, go to Dallas and Chicago. If he talks about which team is built for September, go to Phoenix, San Francisco, and their run differentials. If he points to a lead that actually offers room, go to Houston and ask what to make of five losses to St. Louis.

Use a game, a consequence, and a question. Give Earl room to pick the performance or matchup he wants to explain. Bring in a counterexample if he makes a broad claim, then ask what he would watch next. The prompts below are options for a conversation. Earl’s earlier views come from the August 20 editorial reconstruction in *The Pennant Races Come Home*. Attribute them as positions he took in that feature. Treat his present views as open questions.

## Division notes and follow-ups

### NBC East — Miami got the sweep it needed

The opening briefing put Miami four and a half behind. Four head-to-head wins alone could take four games off that margin. Miami then lost three of four in Atlanta and beat Tampa Bay twice. Charlotte won four of its first six on the road, so Miami actually entered the closing series five and a half back. The Hurricanes won 4–2, 4–3, 5–3 and 6–5. Their return on four wins was a game-and-a-half division deficit and the NBC wild-card position.

**The games to have in mind.** Addie de Ruijter drove in all four Miami runs in the August 27 opener, including a home run (game 1525). Charlotte’s Dabir Aly struck out ten in the loss. On August 30, Pat Watters singled to left with two outs and a one-and-two count in the bottom of the ninth. Dwayne Postell scored the deciding run (game 1561). Across the full challenge de Ruijter had 12 RBI. Miami acquired him from Phoenix on July 29.

**Beyond the leaders.** Atlanta went 8–2, taking three from Miami, splitting with Charlotte and sweeping Tampa Bay. It outscored opponents 61–28. Jordan Santistevan drove in three in each of the first two Tampa Bay games. Tampa Bay’s lone win was 10–9 over Charlotte on August 23. Nate Smeltz drove in five. Walter Kimpton had 12 hits, ten walks and six steals in the window, a specific contribution amid a 1–9 stretch.

**Historical anchor.** Charlotte won the NBC East in 1980 at 91–71. Miami finished 73–89 and already has 71 wins this year. The scheduled September 24–27 rematch is in Charlotte.

**Follow Earl’s answer.** If he still favors Charlotte, ask what the Colonels need from the lineup when they get a start like Aly’s. If he favors Miami, ask whether the four games showed a change he expects to carry into the rematch, and point him to their six-run combined margin. If he treats it as a two-club story, bring up Atlanta taking three of four from Miami before the sweep.

### NBC Central — Dallas did its part and Chicago stayed close

The briefing started Dallas one up on Detroit and two up on Chicago. The Fire took three of four from Detroit and split with Dallas, leaving the Rustlers and Fire tied at 68–59 through August 26. Dallas then swept Detroit in four by a combined 20–4. Chicago won three of four in Minneapolis and ended one back. Detroit’s 1–9 window took it from one game out in the division to six and from the wild-card lead to five and a half behind Miami.

**The games to have in mind.** Heriberto Jimenez struck out 13 and gave Chicago eight scoreless innings in its 1–0 win over Detroit on August 21 (game 1455). Anton Garay allowed four hits in eight scoreless innings in Dallas’s 4–0 win over Detroit on August 29 (game 1551). Garay had given Dallas eight scoreless innings earlier in the challenge, too. Minneapolis won that earlier game 1–0 in ten, with Jose Varela also working eight scoreless innings (game 1480).

**Minneapolis mattered.** It won three of four against Dallas, both games in Detroit and a 14-inning game against Chicago. Hirotoshi Nishimoto’s sacrifice fly brought Ricky Thompson home in that 3–2 win (game 1540). All three of those one-run wins went to extra innings. Chicago’s July 31 acquisition Justin Bowen recorded four saves during the challenge.

**Historical anchor.** Chicago won the Central in 1980. Detroit finished second and won the Grand Series. Through 1980, the Fire had made five postseason trips and were still seeking their first Grand Series title. The August 20 conversation pointed to Chicago’s 11–10 Conference Challenges record despite a plus-27 run differential. In the Division Challenges Chicago went 7–3 while outscoring opponents by six, 40–34. Keep the two windows distinct.

**Follow Earl’s answer.** If he still sees Dallas as the clear choice, ask how he accounts for six head-to-head games left with Chicago. If he credits Chicago’s pitching, go from Jimenez’s start to Bowen’s saves and ask which kind of innings he trusts more in a close race. If he makes a case for Detroit, ask where the Dukes can gain ground first. They open at San Francisco and then go to Charlotte.

### NBC West — Phoenix took first place in San Francisco

Phoenix and San Francisco opened tied at 64–57, with Los Angeles two back. Phoenix took three of four in San Francisco. Los Angeles swept San Diego, won the first game against Phoenix and finished its opening five games 5–0. It lost four of the last five. San Francisco took three of four from the Cobras to stay a game behind Phoenix. The Cobras finished two back, exactly where they began.

**The games to have in mind.** Tony Zacate allowed two hits over eight scoreless innings in Phoenix’s 2–0 win in San Francisco on August 23 (game 1494). He also allowed one earned run in seven and two-thirds innings when Phoenix won 4–1 at San Diego on August 30 (game 1565). San Francisco’s closing wins against Los Angeles included a 15–1 opener and a 10–4 finale in which Bernardo Valenzuela homered and drove in three. Ramon Reyes hit two homers for Los Angeles in the finale (game 1566).

**What Earl was seeing.** San Francisco still had the strongest run differential of the three. It entered the challenge at plus 46 and ended at plus 61. Phoenix moved from minus 23 to minus 16. Los Angeles moved from minus 50 to minus 62. The preceding Conference Challenges told a different story in wins and losses. Phoenix went 13–8, Los Angeles 12–9, and San Francisco 7–14. Put both sets of numbers alongside Phoenix’s actual 3–1 series win at San Francisco. Phoenix won 107 games and the division in 1980. San Diego also affected this race, beating San Francisco once and Phoenix twice after being swept by Los Angeles. Barbu Nica allowed one earned run in 14⅓ innings across two starts in the window.

**Follow Earl’s answer.** If he sticks with San Francisco because of the run totals, ask what evidence from the next Phoenix series would change his mind. If he gives Phoenix the edge, ask what Zacate’s starts showed that the season differential misses. Bring Los Angeles in with a specific question. Can the Cobras use six remaining games against Phoenix to close a two-game gap when they also have four with San Francisco?

### ABC East — Philadelphia won eight in a row and gained one game

The opening briefing counted four games at Boston against a four-game Philadelphia deficit. Boston shut out the Fury 3–0 and 2–0. Philadelphia won the next two 7–2 and 11–2, restoring the original margin. The Fury then won six more against Pittsburgh and New York. Boston won five of its other six, so the margin moved from four to three over the window.

**The players to have in mind.** Jorge Galvez threw a five-hit shutout in Boston’s 2–0 win at Pittsburgh on August 30 (game 1568). He allowed one earned run in 24⅓ innings across the challenge, and Mike Randolph recorded six saves. Ron Stout had 21 hits and 15 RBI in ten games. He went 4-for-5 in Philadelphia’s 10–0 finale against New York, when Gilberto Flores gave the Fury 7⅔ scoreless innings (game 1567).

**Beyond the leaders.** Pittsburgh won this division in 1980 and now sits ten back. Its July acquisition Joe Good gave up one earned run and struck out six in 5⅓ innings in the August 30 loss to Boston. New York scored 12 in its opening win at Pittsburgh and 14 total over nine consecutive losses, including back-to-back shutouts to finish the challenge. Robby Chacon still had 11 hits and six walks in those ten challenge games.

**Follow Earl’s answer.** If he points to Philadelphia’s streak, ask what he expects to hold up at Las Vegas. If he points to Boston’s lead, ask whether the pitching that protected it can do the same in Houston. They have three head-to-head games left, all at Boston September 28–30. Ask how close Philadelphia needs to be when that series opens.

### ABC Central — St. Louis gained six and Houston still has eight

St. Louis completed the unfinished 5–3 win at Houston and swept the four regular games at home. The aggregate was 37–6 across the five. St. Louis also took three of four from Cincinnati, went 9–2 for the best challenge record and drew even with the Cougars at 67–64. Houston went 3–8 and has the ABL’s best record at 75–56. It beat Nashville twice to finish the window and leads Cincinnati and St. Louis by eight.

**The players and games to have in mind.** Chris Jorge threw a six-hit shutout in the 9–0 St. Louis win over Houston on August 23 (game 1498). Danny Martinez had seven hits over the two shutout wins that closed that series. Cincinnati lost to Houston 3–2 in 16 innings on August 24, then Alex Perez struck out ten in 8⅓ scoreless innings in its 4–0 win the next day (games 1506 and 1516). Houston’s 10–9 finale against Nashville ended with the bases loaded when Juan Grant lined out to center. The potential Nashville go-ahead run was on second (game 1569).

**Historical anchor.** Houston won four consecutive ABC pennants from 1977 through 1980 and won the 1979 Grand Series. Cincinnati won the Central at 97–65 last year. Houston ended all four of Cincinnati’s prior postseason trips, most recently in the 1980 DCS. St. Louis finished 62–100 last year and already has 67 wins this year. Nashville split with all three rivals, and Jose Orozco drove in 14 in ten games.

**Follow Earl’s answer.** If he says the division still runs through Houston, ask which parts of the club make an eight-game lead durable, then ask what he saw in those five losses to St. Louis. If he puts St. Louis back in the race, ask which route he would watch first. The Stallions and Cougars are both eight behind Houston and four off the wild card. St. Louis visits Cincinnati September 24–27 and then hosts Houston September 28–30.

### ABC West — Denver’s last two wins left all three within a game

Las Vegas started three up on Denver and five up on Seattle. Seattle won three of four against Las Vegas and two at Portland. It shared first with Las Vegas through August 29. Denver lost its first three to Portland, won the fourth, swept two from Las Vegas and dropped the first two at Seattle by a run apiece. Denver won the last two there 4–2 and 2–1. Las Vegas’s 5–2 win at Portland on August 30 put it one up on both clubs.

**The games to have in mind.** In the 14th on August 21, Seattle’s David Olguin stole second, took third on the throwing error and scored on Adam Wilson’s single to beat Las Vegas 4–3 (game 1476). On August 30, Denver’s Damian Serano struck out seven in 5⅔ innings. Seattle’s Tony Miller struck out eight in 7⅓. Denver won 2–1 (game 1572). These were two different one-run games with consequences for Seattle. The Comets went 4–2 in one-run games during the challenges and 7–3 overall.

**Beyond the leaders.** Portland won three at Denver and split four with Las Vegas. Jeremy Campbell hit seven homers and drove in 13 in ten games. Three of those homers came in consecutive Portland wins over Las Vegas on August 28–29. Las Vegas won this division in 1980. Denver and Seattle both missed the tournament that year.

**Follow Earl’s answer.** If he stays with Las Vegas, ask how much of its one-game lead he would count on with six Denver games and three Seattle games remaining. If he sticks with Denver for the wild card, ask what winning the last two in Seattle showed him that June or July could not. If he prefers Seattle, ask which of its close wins he would use to explain how it can take first. Then bring the question back to the schedule. Denver hosts Seattle for four before Las Vegas goes to Seattle for three.

**If the season ended on these records.** The 1981 Division Championship Series (DCS) rule keeps the No. 1 division winner and the wild card apart when they come from the same division. The pairings then become No. 1 vs. No. 3 and No. 2 vs. No. 4. In the NBC, Charlotte would be No. 1 and Miami No. 4. Charlotte would face Phoenix, and Dallas would face Miami. In the ABC, Houston would face whichever of Denver or Seattle ultimately took the tied wild-card spot, and Las Vegas would face Boston. That is an August positioning exercise. Actual qualification and pairings depend on the remaining games and any official tiebreak.

**A question that opens the bracket discussion.** Of the four clubs currently within half a game of a wild-card spot or holding it, which should be thinking division first? Use Miami and Chicago as examples in one conference and Denver and Seattle in the other. Keep final qualification and projected matchups conditional.

## Ways to keep the conversation moving

- **Ask for a game when Earl gives a verdict.** Which game changed his mind about a club? If he points to the standings, ask for the inning or the decision that made the change visible.
- **When Earl emphasizes outcomes, change the scale.** Philadelphia won eight straight and gained one game. Chicago went 7–3 with a six-run surplus. St. Louis beat Houston five times and still trails by eight. Ask which example tells him the most about September and why.
- **When Earl favors the established club, give the challenger a specific reply.** Phoenix won in San Francisco. Miami won all four against Charlotte. Seattle won three of four from Las Vegas. Ask what one September result would make him revise his position.
- **Bring in the clubs farther back through what they did.** Atlanta shaped the East, Minneapolis took games from three Central clubs, and Portland altered the West while Campbell hit seven home runs. Ask which of those opponents a contender least wants to see now.
- **Close with a choice and a condition.** If Earl could watch one of the September rematches, which would he choose? What has to happen between now and first pitch for it to decide something? Let his choice be the last word.
