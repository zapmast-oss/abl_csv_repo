# July 20 Stakes-First Framing Notes

**Purpose:** Voice and framing pass only  
**Data cutoff:** Games completed through July 19, 1981  
**Ranking status:** Unchanged

## Editorial Correction

Baseball matters because the stakes are incredibly high. Begin with what can change, then use the records to prove why it can change.

The July 20 board is not simply a list of standings. It is a pressure map.

## Framing Order

1. **Consequence:** What ground can be gained or lost?
2. **Pressure:** Who must protect separation, and who can close it?
3. **Opportunity and danger:** What can this game or series change?
4. **Proof:** Records, margins, schedule, and validated results.
5. **Enrichment:** Clearly labeled StatsPlus context.
6. **Boundary:** What the evidence does not authorize.

## Story Applications

### Dallas–Detroit

Hook: Dallas has only two games of separation to protect. Detroit is close enough to take ground immediately.

Proof: Dallas 52–41; Detroit 50–43. This remains the lead race-board story.

Enrichment: Dallas has 60.0% division odds and Detroit 24.9%; ELO is essentially even. Detroit attendance and fan interest establish public scale, not emotion.

### Chicago–Dallas

Hook: Chicago cannot take the lead in this series, but it can erase the gap. A sweep ties Dallas. A Dallas sweep pushes Chicago eight games back. That is the sizzle.

Proof: Chicago is 48–45, four games behind 52–41 Dallas, with four Dallas-at-Chicago games scheduled July 20–23. Chicago earned a 2–2 road split at Detroit and won 4–1 on July 19.

Enrichment: Dallas has 60.0% division odds to Chicago's 15.0%. ELO is close and BaseRuns places both near expected record. Dallas remains the model favorite.

Boundary: Chicago cannot take sole first place in the series. Five playoff appearances without a title add franchise context, not assumed motivation or burden.

### Broader Race Board

Hook: Pressure is not isolated. Two, three, four, and five games of separation are being defended across four other divisions.

Proof: San Francisco–Phoenix is two games; Las Vegas–Seattle three; Boston–New York four; Houston–Cincinnati five.

Boundary: Do not call any race settled or convert odds into qualification.

### Las Vegas at Denver

Hook: The schedule puts standings pressure directly on the field—a division leader visits a 50-win divisional opponent.

Proof: Las Vegas 54–39; Denver 50–43; scheduled July 20 and unplayed in this material.

Enrichment: Odds, ELO, BaseRuns, WAR, and UBR may describe the matchup. They do not prove a result or a causal baserunning edge.

### Player Value

Hook: The pressure is continuation. Coronado and Flores must sustain the value cases they have built.

Proof: Use the approved WAR, ERA, innings, home-run, and plate-appearance lines.

Enrichment: Label Coronado's rWAR/FIP/xFIP and Flores's wRC+/wOBA as StatsPlus. Do not declare awards.

### Watch and Hold

Hook: Pressure can be noticed before it earns a full story.

Atlanta and Seattle remain watch conditions. Detroit attendance and Tampa Bay financial context remain descriptive. Philadelphia remains held.

Boundary: No turnaround, collapse, luck, guaranteed regression, injury causation, fan emotion, or organizational blame without additional evidence.

## Language Guide

Prefer:

- “Dallas has two games of separation to protect.”
- “Chicago has ground to gain and just as much to lose.”
- “The series can erase the gap or double it.”
- “The standings prove where the pressure sits.”
- “The schedule creates the opportunity; the game supplies the answer.”

Avoid:

- Opening with a table of records.
- “Chicago can take the division lead.”
- “The season is over” or “the division is decided.”
- “Due for regression,” “choked,” “collapsed,” or “must feel.”
- Treating StatsPlus odds as results or official standings.

## StatsPlus Doctrine

StatsPlus enhances. It does not replace. Playoff odds, ELO, BaseRuns, injuries, fan/financial context, UBR, and rWAR must be labeled as enrichment. Owner/front-office traits and best-game discovery remain reference-only.

## Final Voice Test

Before delivery, ask:

- Does the section begin with consequence rather than arithmetic?
- Do the records prove the hook instead of becoming the hook?
- Is every dramatic word earned by a measurable stake?
- Is the next game allowed to prove the story?
